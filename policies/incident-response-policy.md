Incident Response Policy: 



1. Purpose:




The purpose of this Incident Response Policy is to ensure Arthur Financial Services can quickly detect, report, analyze, contain, and recover from cybersecurity incidents. This policy establishes clear responsibilities, communication channels, escalation procedures, and documentation requirements.

2. Scope:



This policy applies to:

All employees, contractors, interns, and third-party service providers.



All company systems including Microsoft 365, Microsoft Entra ID, Windows laptops, cloud storage, VPN, and customer financial data.



3. Definitions:



Security Incident: Any event that threatens confidentiality, integrity, or availability of company systems or data.



Examples: phishing attacks, malware infections, unauthorized access, data loss, suspicious login activity, compromised accounts, or VPN misuse.





4. Reporting a Suspected Incident



All staff must immediately report suspected incidents through one of the following channels:

Email: security@arthurfinancialservices.com

Phone: 1800-123-4567

Microsoft 365 Security Portal: “Report an Incident” form or Phishing: Use the Microsoft “Report Phishing” button.



Employees must report incidents as soon as they are detected, including:



Suspicious emails, Unexpected MFA prompts, Unauthorized access attempts, Lost or stolen devices, Data exposure or misdelivery or Abnormal system behavior.



Failure to report incidents may result in disciplinary action.




5. Roles and Responsibilities



5.1 Incident Response Coordinator (IRC)



The IRC (typically the Security Manager or CISO) is responsible for:

Leading and coordinating the incident response, Assigning tasks to IT Security and Support teams, Escalating critical issues to executive leadership, Approving containment and recovery actions and Ensuring documentation is completed.



5.2 IT Security Team


Investigates alerts from Microsoft 365, Entra ID, and endpoint protection, Performs containment actions (account lockouts, device isolation, etc.), Collects logs and evidence, Supports recovery and remediation and Documents all actions taken.



5.3 IT Support Team



Assists with device isolation, password resets, and user communication, Helps restore affected systems and Implements technical fixes recommended by IT Security.



5.4 Executive Leadership



Makes decisions for severe incidents involving customer data, legal exposure, or business continuity.



Approves public communication and regulatory notifications.



6. Escalation Levels



Level 1 – Low Severity: Minor issues with no data exposure.


Handled by IT Support.


Examples: accidental email to wrong internal user, minor malware blocked automatically.




Level 2 – Medium Severity: Potential unauthorized access or service disruption.


Handled by IT Security.


Examples: suspicious login attempts, repeated MFA failures, phishing clicks.




Level 3 – High Severity: Confirmed unauthorized access, data exposure, or system compromise.


Escalated to IRC and executive leadership.


Examples: compromised admin account, ransomware attempt, VPN misuse.

Level 4 – Critical Severity: Major breach affecting customer financial data or business operations.


Requires full incident response activation, legal review, and regulatory reporting.


Examples: large-scale data breach, widespread malware infection, cloud storage compromise.
